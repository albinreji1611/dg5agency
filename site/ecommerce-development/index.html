WHAT ECOMMERCE DEVELOPMENT ACTUALLY MEANS HERE

A store is a checkout wearing a nice theme.

Most ecommerce projects get judged on how the homepage looks in a pitch deck. We judge them on what happens three clicks later: does the product page load in under two seconds on a mid range Android phone on a patchy connection, does the size selector actually work on mobile, does Razorpay confirm the payment without dropping the order, and does the whole thing stay upright when a Diwali sale sends five times your usual traffic in an hour. Ecommerce development is the discipline of getting every one of those moments right, not just the ones a client sees in a Figma file.

We build on Shopify and on WooCommerce, and we pick the platform based on your catalog, your margins, and how much control you need over checkout, not on which one we happen to prefer building. Either way the build covers the same ground: a fast, mobile first storefront, a clean catalog and inventory setup, a checkout wired to the payment methods Indian shoppers actually use, and a measurable lift in conversion rate, not just a store that technically exists.

What we actually do differently

Most ecommerce agencies hand over a theme, connect a payment gateway, and call the project done. We load test the checkout under simulated sale day traffic before launch, audit product page speed on 3G, and stay on for the first thirty days to fix whatever the launch traffic actually breaks, not just what we guessed might break.

  • Platform chosen on merit — Shopify or WooCommerce, decided against your catalog size and margins, not a house preference.
  • Checkout built for India — Razorpay, UPI, and card flows tested with real bank redirects, not just a sandbox key.
  • Speed budget enforced — product and category pages held to a load time target on real mobile networks, not a lab score.
  • Sale day load tested — checkout and inventory verified under simulated traffic spikes before launch, not discovered live.

Who this is for.

D2C and fashion brands moving off a marketplace-only model, retailers with a physical catalog going online for the first time, and growing stores that have outgrown a template theme and need real checkout and catalog engineering underneath.

Who this isn't for.

Brands wanting a store thrown up in a weekend with no thought to catalog structure or checkout reliability. A store built that way tends to fail exactly when a sale finally sends it real traffic.

HOW AN ECOMMERCE BUILD RUNS

From platform decision to a launch that holds up, in five stages.

01
Platform & catalog audit.

We map your SKU count, variant complexity, shipping rules, and expected order volume, then recommend Shopify or WooCommerce with the reasoning laid out, not assumed.

02
Design & theme build.

A storefront built mobile first, with product and category templates designed around how people actually browse and compare on a phone.

03
Catalog & checkout wiring.

Product data migrated or entered, inventory synced, and Razorpay, UPI, and card checkout connected and tested with real transactions, not just a sandbox pass.

04
Speed & load testing.

Product and checkout pages benchmarked on real mobile networks, then checkout load tested against simulated sale day traffic before we let you launch on it.

05
Launch & thirty day watch.

We stay on for the first month after go live, watching checkout completion and page speed under real traffic and fixing whatever launch actually surfaces.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Common ecommerce build questions, answered upfront.

YOU10:31
Should we build on Shopify or WooCommerce?
DG510:31
It depends on your catalog and how much control you want. Shopify suits merchants who want a managed platform, fast launch, and predictable uptime, with a monthly platform fee attached. WooCommerce suits merchants who want full ownership of hosting and checkout logic and are comfortable with more setup work upfront. We'll recommend one against your actual catalog, not a default preference.
YOU10:33
How do you handle Razorpay and UPI setup?
DG510:33
We integrate Razorpay directly, which covers UPI, cards, netbanking, and wallets through a single gateway, and we test the full flow with real bank redirects before launch, not just a sandbox key. If you already have a merchant account with another gateway, we can usually wire that in instead.
YOU10:35
Can you migrate our existing catalog and orders?
DG510:35
Yes. We migrate products, variants, images, and historical order data from most existing platforms, and we audit the catalog structure during migration rather than moving bad data as is, since a messy catalog causes more support tickets than almost anything else.
YOU10:37
Will the store hold up during a big sale?
DG510:37
That is exactly what we load test for before launch. We simulate concentrated traffic against your checkout and product pages ahead of any sale you plan, and flag anything that needs scaling, whether that is hosting, inventory locking, or a third-party app that chokes under load.
